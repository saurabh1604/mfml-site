         The proof of the mean-value theorem comes from Rolle’s
         theorem whose statement follows. We shall show that the
         development of Taylor’s series involves the repeated
         application of Rolle’s theorem below:
         Theorem: If f is a continuous function on [a, b] and
         differentiable on (a, b) with f (a) = f (b) = 0, then there
         exists some c in (a, b) such that f ′ (c ) = 0.

          A necessary and sufficient criterion of positive-definiteness
          for a Hermitian matrix (such as the Hessian matrix) is
          Sylvester’s criterion - the determinant of every upper left
          m × m submatrix should be positive which means that

          fxx > 0 and fxx fyy − fxy > 0 at (a, b). This condition
          corresponds to bullet point 2 on Slide 15.
          Note that the Hessian matrix is symmetric, so that in case
          of a local minimum it is a symmetric, positive-definite matrix
          which we know from the linear algebra part of this course is
          one that has positive eigenvalues.
