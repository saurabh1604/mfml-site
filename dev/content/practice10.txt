 The part-(c) twist – transforming an unseen point with training statistics – is where marks

   Problem 4 (8 marks) MODERATE COMPUTATION

 Consider L(x, y) = 9x2 + y 2 .
  (a) Write the GD updates for x and y as multiplications by constant factors, and find the
      exact range of γ for convergence.                                                           [3]
  (b) With γ = 0.1, trace three iterations from (x0 , y0 ) = (1, 3). Which coordinate oscillates and
      why?                                                                                        [3]
  (c) Compute the Hessian and its condition number. What curvature profile would make GD
      fastest, and which single γ then finishes in one step?                                      [2]


 Step-by-step solution


 Step 1 (decoupled updates). The gradient is ∇L = (18x, 2y), so

                              xk+1 = (1 − 18γ) xk ,    yk+1 = (1 − 2γ) yk .

 Stability needs both factors inside (−1, 1):
                                           2
                       |1 − 18γ| < 1 ⇒ γ < 18 = 19 ,         |1 − 2γ| < 1 ⇒ γ < 1.

 The stiff direction binds:
                                           0 < γ < 19 ≈ 0.111




                                               Page 4 of 8
ZC416 MFML/MFDS                                              Lecture 10 – Optimization: Exam Practice



 Step 2 (trace with γ = 0.1). Factors: 1 − 1.8 = −0.8 for x; 1 − 0.2 = 0.8 for y:

                   (1, 3) → (−0.8, 2.4) → (0.64, 1.92) → (−0.512, 1.536).

 Both coordinates shrink by 20% per step, but x flips sign each iteration: its factor is neg-
 ative because γ = 0.1 exceeds 1/18 ≈ 0.056, the smooth-descent threshold for that coordinate.
 Each step overshoots the valley floor in the stiff direction and bounces back, while y descends
 monotonically – zig-zag in its cleanest form.

 Step 3 (Hessian and the ideal bowl).
                                      
                                  18 0                     18
                            H=           ,            κ=      = 9.
                                  0 2                       2

 GD is fastest when curvature is moderate and equal in all directions (κ = 1, a circular bowl, e.g.
 9x2 + 9y 2 ): the gradient then points straight at the minimizer, and γ = 1/λ (here 1/18) sends
 both coordinates exactly to 0 in one step. With κ = 9 no such γ exists – one-step convergence
 would need γ = 1/18 and γ = 1/2 simultaneously.

  Answer. (a) factors (1 − 18γ), (1 − 2γ); range γ < 1/9; (b) (−0.8, 2.4) → (0.64, 1.92) →
  (−0.512, 1.536); x oscillates (negative factor); (c) H = diag(18, 2), κ = 9; equal moderate
  curvature is ideal, then γ = 1/λ finishes in one step.

 Intuition & exam tips

 Everything about differential curvature in one computation: per-coordinate factors (1 − γλi ),
 the stiffest eigenvalue capping γ at 2/λmax , the sign of a factor determining oscillation, and
 κ = λmax /λmin as the difficulty score. When asked “why is the elliptical bowl slow?” – answer
 with the two factors, not with prose.

   Problem 6 (6 marks) DIFFICULT COMPUTATION

 For F (x) = 3x4 − 4x3 − 12x2 + 5, small-step gradient descent converges to the local minimum
 at x = −1 from any start x0 < 0, and to the global minimum at x = 2 from any start x0 > 0
 (the watershed – a local maximum – sits at x = 0). The initial point x0 is drawn uniformly from
 [−2, 3].
   (a) Compute the probability that a single run converges to the global minimum.              [2]
   (b) With k independent restarts (keeping the best result), find the smallest k for which the
       global minimum is found with probability ≥ 0.99.                                        [3]
   (c) If a separable 10-dimensional objective has such a landscape in every coordinate (per-
       coordinate success probability 0.6, independent), what is the chance that a single run
       lands in the global basin of all coordinates simultaneously?                            [1]


 Step-by-step solution


 Step 1 (one run). Success requires x0 > 0 (from x0 < 0 the run is funnelled to −1; the
 single point x0 = 0 has probability zero under a continuous draw). Favourable length over total
 length:
                                            3−0      3
                                     p=            = = 0.6.
                                          3 − (−2)   5

 Step 2 (restarts). One run fails with probability 0.4. We need

                               1 − 0.4k ≥ 0.99     ⇐⇒       0.4k ≤ 0.01.

 Compute the powers:

                       0.45 = 0.01024 > 0.01,         0.46 = 0.004096 ≤ 0.01.

 So k = 6 restarts (success probability ≈ 0.9959; k = 5 just misses at 0.98976).

 Step 3 (ten coordinates at once). By independence:

                                      P = 0.610 ≈ 0.006 (≈ 0.6%).

 Even with per-coordinate odds well above one half, the joint success collapses exponentially
 – the high-dimensional version of the local-minima trap, and the reason restarts alone cannot
 rescue GD in ML.

  Answer. (a) p = 0.6; (b) k = 6; (c) 0.610 ≈ 0.6%.

 Intuition & exam tips

 The template 1 − (1 − p)k (“at least one success in k tries”) plus a careful power computation is
 a recurring exam device. Basins multiply across coordinates, so probabilities multiply too. Note
 how the numbers are engineered so that k = 5 barely fails – compute powers precisely rather
 than rounding early.
