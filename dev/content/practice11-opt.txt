  Question 1                                                                                6 marks

  Gradient descent on a two-parameter regression loss, and recovering a learning rate
  A straight line ŷ = wx + b is fitted to the two points ( x, y) = (1, 3) and (2, 5) by minimizing
  the Mean Squared Error

                                         1h                             i
                             L(w, b) =      (w + b − 3)2 + (2w + b − 5)2 .
                                         2
   (a) Derive ∂L/∂w and ∂L/∂b in their simplest form, and write the gradient-descent update
       equations.                                                                      [1.5]

   (b) Starting from (w0 , b0 ) = (0, 0) with α = 0.1, perform two iterations. Report (w1 , b1 ),
       (w2 , b2 ) and the loss after each.                                               [2.5]

   (c) A classmate started from the same point (0, 0) with a different learning rate and after one
       iteration reached (w1 , b1 ) = (0.26, 0.16). Determine the learning rate they used, and verify
       it with both coordinates.                                                                  [1]

   (d) Using the Hessian of L, decide whether α = 0.3 would have been a safe learning rate.
       Justify.                                                                        [1]


  How to attempt this question

  What is being tested. Turning a loss into a gradient (chain rule); running the update rule
  without slips; understanding that one gradient-descent step is “α times the gradient vector”,
  so a landing point reveals α; and the stability rule α < 2/λmax .
  Plan of attack.
   1. Do not expand the squares. Differentiate each squared term with the chain rule,
       d        2             d(stuff)
      dw (stuff) = 2(stuff) ·   dw , then collect like terms.

   2. For each iteration, in this order: gradient at the current point → update both parameters
      with that same gradient → loss at the new point.

   3. “Find the learning rate”: write one step with α unknown, (w1 , b1 ) = (w0 − αgw , b0 − αgb );
      solve from one coordinate, check with the other.

   4. Hessian = matrix of second partials. Eigenvalues from λ2 − (trace)λ + det = 0. Safe iff
      α < 2/λmax .

 STEP 7   Apply the stability rule                                                             part (d)

On a quadratic, each step multiplies the error along an eigen-direction by (1 − αλ). Convergence
needs every such factor to have magnitude below 1, i.e. α < 2/λmax :

                                                  2
                                       αmax =         ≈ 0.292.
                                                6.854
With α = 0.3 the factor would be 1 − 0.3(6.854) = −1.056: the error flips sign and grows by 5.6%

   Answer (d). Not safe: 0.3 > 2/λmax ≈ 0.292, so gradient descent would diverge along the

direction (factor 1 − 0.1(0.146) = 0.985 per step) — the classic reason plain gradient descent is slow
on real regression problems.

  Three habits these questions are training

   1. Whenever an update rule is given, the first step is almost always trivial because every
      accumulator is zero — use it to pin down α, to sanity-check your arithmetic, or to see what
      the method “really” does.

   2. On any quadratic, one step multiplies each error component by 1 − αλ: this single fact
      settles convergence, oscillation, divergence and the safe range of α.

   3. AdaGrad,
             p RMSProp and Adam are all gradient descent with a per-coordinate learning
      rate α/ (average of past g2 ); they differ only in which average (all history, a decaying
      window, or a bias-corrected decaying window plus momentum).
