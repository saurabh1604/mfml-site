## Sections (ids stable; ~16 + practice). Each: Imagine this → tiny example → widget(s) with "Try:" → The realization → checks → `<!--@ALG sid-->` drawer (where listed) → In one sentence.

**Act I — the widest street**
- **s1 · Which line should you draw?** Many lines have zero training error; training error cannot choose; the next point lands near an old one; pick the widest empty street. Robustness, uniqueness, generalisation, sparsity (companion ch.1). **w-tilt**: a 2-D dataset; a tilt dial; for each tilt the street is pushed out until it touches a point on each side (width readout, touching points ringed); a width-vs-tilt curve with the best tilt marked; "▶ find the widest". Ends with the numbered roadmap.
- **s2 · The boundary's compass: w and b.** w ⟂ boundary (subtract two boundary points), b only slides it; signed distance f(z)/‖w‖; functional vs geometric margin (table). **w-probe**: drag a probe point; dashed perpendicular to the line x₁ + 2x₂ = 6 (as in the companion; w = (1,2), ‖w‖ = √5 ≈ 2.236); readouts raw score f, ‖w‖, distance f/‖w‖, side; knobs for w's angle and b showing b slides without turning. Checks. Drawer: both proofs.
- **s3 · How wide is the street?** Push two parallel planes w·x + b = ±c until they touch; subtract → w·(x⁺ − x⁻) = 2c; Cauchy–Schwarz ⇒ gap 2c/‖w‖ (and the "you cannot divide by a vector" trap). **w-gap**: two points each sliding along its own edge; the perpendicular gap stays pinned; the point-to-point distance only equals it when the joining line is parallel to w. Checks. Drawer.
- **s4 · The scale is yours to choose.** Multiply (w, b, c) by k — the planes don't move, every number does; only 2c/‖w‖ is real; choose c = 1 (canonical); margin 2/‖w‖; one inequality yᵢ(w·xᵢ + b) ≥ 1 replaces two (why labels are ±1). Why not maximise the raw score (k → ∞). **w-scale (3-D)**: the slab in ℝ³ from the companion (support vectors on x₁+x₂+x₃ = 6 and = 2; boundary = 4; w = (½,½,½), b = −2; thickness 4/√3 ≈ 2.31), with a k slider: all readouts (c, ‖w‖, scores) scale, the geometry and the width stay frozen; "look edge-on" button collapses to the 2-D picture. Checks.
- **s5 · The problem, stated — and why we square.** max 2/‖w‖ s.t. yᵢ(w·xᵢ+b) ≥ 1 ⇔ min ½‖w‖²; three rewrites that keep the winner; the right reason for squaring (‖w‖ has a kink at 0; ½‖w‖² is smooth, gradient w); convex QP → one bottom, reproducible; support vectors are an output; hard margin has a hard failure mode (one overlapping point → no solution at all). **w-qp (3-D)**: the bowl ½‖w‖² over the (w₁, w₂) plane for a small dataset with b fixed at its optimum; each data point's constraint drawn as a glass wall/half-plane cut on the floor; the feasible region glows; a ball rolls to the lowest feasible point = the SVM; toggle "add an overlapping point" → feasible region vanishes. Plus a small 1-D panel comparing ‖w‖, ½‖w‖², 2/‖w‖ (all optimised at the same feasible point). Checks.

**Act II — prices on points: the machinery**
- **s6 · Every point gets a price tag: the Lagrangian and KKT.** L = ½‖w‖² − Σαᵢ[yᵢ(w·xᵢ+b) − 1], αᵢ ≥ 0; min-max reading (violated → +∞, slack → α = 0, tight → free); α as how hard a point pushes; the four KKT conditions table; the sign-convention trap. Link to Unit 11 §8–§11. **w-push**: a solved 2-D SVM; each point shows a pressure bar (αᵢ); drag a non-support point around — its α stays 0 and nothing moves until it touches the street; drag a support vector — the street re-solves live (use a small exact solver — see "Solver" below). Checks.
- **s7 · The boundary is built from the data.** Stationarity: w = Σαᵢyᵢxᵢ and Σαᵢyᵢ = 0 (the pushes on each side balance — a tug-of-war). **w-build**: arrows αᵢyᵢxᵢ laid tip-to-tail summing to w (animated), balance beam showing Σαᵢyᵢ = 0. Drawer: Lemma A (∇½‖w‖² = w), Lemma B, ∂L/∂w, ∂L/∂b.
- **s8 · The dual: a problem only in α.** Substitute back: max Σαᵢ − ½ΣΣαᵢαⱼyᵢyⱼ(xᵢ·xⱼ), s.t. αᵢ ≥ 0, Σαᵢyᵢ = 0. Two points (1,1)⁺, (−1,−1)⁻: dual 2α − 4α² → α = ¼, w = (½,½), b = 0, margin 2√2 = the distance between the points; strong duality: primal ½‖w‖² = ¼ = dual value (a free check). **w-dual (3-D)**: the dual objective surface over (α₁, α₂) with the constraint line α₁ = α₂ drawn on it; the peak; a second tab with three points where the surface lives on a plane slice. Checks. Drawer: the substitution.
- **s9 · Support vectors: the few points holding up the street.** Complementary slackness ⇒ three regions (outside: α = 0; on the edge: α ≥ 0 — the "on the edge but α = 0" trap; inside: impossible in hard margin). Sparsity: delete every non-support point and nothing changes; predict with SVs only. **w-sparse — the flagship widget**: a live 2-D SVM on ~20 points with a real solver; click to add points of either class, drag, delete; the street re-solves in real time; SVs ringed with their α; a "delete every non-support point" button proves nothing moves; a counter "the answer depends on k of n points". Checks.

**Act III — the messy world**
- **s10 · Soft margin: allowing a few trespassers.** yᵢ(w·xᵢ+b) ≥ 1 − ξᵢ, ξᵢ ≥ 0 and the four readings of ξ (table + picture); the fine C; min ½‖w‖² + CΣξᵢ; the one change in the dual: 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C (why the cap is the robustness); reading C (small C: wide street, more SVs; large C: narrow street, C → ∞ = hard margin; runs opposite to ridge's λ); the three α regimes (0 / free 0<α<C / bounded α=C) and b only from a free SV. **w-slack**: the companion's scenario (one rogue positive); a log-scale C dial 0.01 … 1000; street width, total slack, each point's ξ printed next to it, colour by regime; side-by-side "small C vs large C" snapshots. Checks. Drawer: the soft-margin dual in outline (the αᵢ + µᵢ = C step).
- **s11 · The hinge loss falls out of the slack.** ξᵢ = max(0, 1 − zᵢ) with zᵢ = yᵢ(w·xᵢ+b); the table (z = 2, 1, 0.5, 0, −0.5, −2 → loss 0, 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 3); regulariser + loss form; why hinge beats counting mistakes (flat-with-a-cliff vs a slope). **w-hinge**: drag a point across the street; its z marker slides along the hinge curve with the 0/1 loss drawn for comparison. Checks.

**Act IV — the kernel trick, built from the base**
- **s12 · Dot products are everywhere — and the classifier is a vote.** The data enters the dual and f(x) = Σαᵢyᵢ(xᵢ·x) + b only through dot products; f as a vote: importance αᵢ × side yᵢ × similarity (xᵢ·x) + offset b. **w-vote**: a trained SVM; drag a new point; each SV shows its vote bar (α·y·similarity) stacking to f with b; the final sign lights the predicted class. Checks.
- **s13 · When no straight line will do: lift the data.** 1-D: positives at ±1, negatives at ±2 — add x² → (±1,1), (±2,4), cut at x² = 2.5 → back down, two thresholds ±√2.5. XOR: add x₁x₂ → one class rises to +1, the other sinks to −1, plane x₃ = 0 → back down, the axes. Rings: add x₁² + x₂² → a paraboloid → a flat cut → a circle. **w-lift (3-D, three tabs)**: animated lift of each dataset from the floor into 3-D, a glass plane slicing it, and its shadow on the floor drawn as the curved boundary. The owner's line: "SVM never stopped being linear; it is linear in the higher space."
- **s14 · The trick: similarity without ever visiting the big space.** φ(x) = (x₁², √2x₁x₂, x₂²) ⇒ φ(x)·φ(z) = (x·z)² step by step, why the √2; kernel families table (linear; polynomial homogeneous (x·z)^d; non-homogeneous (x·z+1)^d — 6 features for d = 2: (x₁², x₂², √2x₁x₂, √2x₁, √2x₂, 1); RBF — infinite-dimensional; Mercer in one friendly paragraph); how many features we secretly use: C(n+d−1, d) and C(n+d, d) (n = 10, d = 4 → 715 and 1001; n = 2, d = 2 → 3 and 6). **w-kernel**: a calculator — type/drag x and z; both routes computed side by side (build φ, dot them / dot, then square) and they match; kernel selector; feature-count panel with n, d sliders and the "cost of building φ vs cost of the kernel" bars. Checks: (1,2)·(3,1) = 5 → 25 both ways; counts.
- **s15 · A kernel SVM, start to finish.** The exam recipe (8 steps: kernel matrix — dots first, kernel second; look for symmetry; Σαy = 0; write the dual with numbers; check α ≥ 0; b from a support vector; classify; optional: simplify f to see the curve). Worked: x₁ = (1,2)⁺, x₂ = (−1,−2)⁺, x₃ = (1,−2)⁻, x₄ = (−1,2)⁻, K = (xᵀz)²: dot table row 1 = 5, −5, −3, 3 → K row 25, 25, 9, 9; each row of yᵢyⱼKᵢⱼ sums to 32; all α equal a; dual 4a − 64a² → a = 1/32; b = 0; f(x) = x₁x₂/2 — the boundary is the pair of axes; classify a new point (e.g. (2, 1) → f = 1 → +1). **w-playground — the second flagship**: preset datasets (XNOR 4-point, rings, two moons, overlapping blobs, the 1-D ±1/±2 lifted), kernel selector (linear / (xᵀz)² / (xᵀz+1)² / RBF with γ), C slider (soft margin), live solver, decision regions shaded with contour f = 0 and f = ±1, SVs ringed with α; a "show the recipe" panel that, for the 4-point preset, walks the 8 steps with live numbers. Checks.
