### Act I — Look back instead of remembering
**s1 · The bottleneck, and the fix: let the decoder look back.** New lead section (replaces the current s1, keep its relay-vs-direct widget **w-relay** if it still earns its place). Unit 17's decoder had to write a whole Hindi sentence from one summary vector. Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio (2015): keep *all* the encoder's notes, and before writing each output word let the decoder **score** every encoder note, turn the scores into shares with softmax, and take the **weighted blend** (the *context vector*). Anchor: **an interpreter who glances back at the speaker's notes before each word, looking hardest at the part that matters now.** Worked (dot-product scoring, Luong et al. 2015): encoder notes I = (1, 0, 0), drink = (0, 1, 0), tea = (0, 0, 1); the decoder's state while writing "चाय" (chai) is q = (0, 0.5, 2) → scores **(0, 0.5, 2)** → weights **(0.100, 0.164, 0.736)** → context **(0.100, 0.164, 0.736)**: it looks mostly at "tea". The **alignment map**: "I drink tea" → "मैं चाय पीता हूँ" (main chai peeta hoon) — Hindi puts the verb at the end, so the bright cells bend: चाय ↔ tea, पीता ↔ drink (label the map as a hand-made illustration). Scoring variants compared: dot (qᵀh), general (qᵀWh), **additive** (Bahdanau: vᵀ tanh(W₁h + W₂s)); tiny additive example with W₁ = W₂ = I, v = (1, 1), s = (0.5, −0.5), h = (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1): scores **(0.443, 0.924, 1.367)** → weights **(0.195, 0.315, 0.491)**. Widget (new): **w-align** — step through the decoder writing मैं / चाय / पीता / हूँ; at each step gold beams from the decoder state to the three English notes (thickness = weight), the context vector assembled, and the alignment matrix filling in row by row; a toggle "no attention (Unit 17)" that shows the single summary instead.

**s5 · The payoff: one word, many meanings.** New. Unit 16's "bat"/"bank" problem dissolves: the output of self-attention for a word depends on its sentence. ### Act III — Order and depth: the transformer block
**s8 · Where is each word? Three ways to add position.** Keep order-blindness (**w-shuffle**: shuffle the words, each word's answer moves with it — attention treats a sentence as a bag). Compare three fixes side by side (table + widget tabs): **learned position vectors** (a lookup row per position; simple; cannot go past the longest training length), **sinusoidal** (clock hands at different speeds; d = 4 values: pos 1 = (0.8415, 0.5403, 0.0100, 1.0000), pos 2 = (0.9093, −0.4161, 0.0200, 0.9998), pos 3 = (0.1411, −0.9900, 0.0300, 0.9996); key property: PE(1)·PE(2) = PE(2)·PE(3) = **1.5403** — the dot product depends only on the distance), **rotary / RoPE** (rotate q and k by angles proportional to position; θ = 30°, q = k = (1, 0): positions (3,1), (5,3), (2,0) all give q·k = cos 60° = **0.5** — only the gap matters). Widgets **w-clocks**, **w-rope** (keep; check), plus a small comparison tab in one of them.

**s9 · The transformer block.** Keep the tower (**w-block**), layer norm (**w-layernorm**: (1, 2, 3, 6) → mean 3, variance 3.5 → **(−1.069, −0.535, 0, 1.604)**) and the parameter count for d = 512, d_ff = 2 048 (weights only **3 145 728**; + biases **3 150 336**; + two layer norms **3 152 384**). Deepen with three anchor ideas: the **residual stream** is a shared notebook every sub-layer reads and adds a note to (and it is the same express-lane trick as the LSTM's cell state — blame flows straight back); **attention = the words talk to each other, feed-forward = each word thinks on its own** (a small per-word network, 4× wider, GELU or ReLU); layer norm keeps every word's numbers on the same scale. Mention pre-norm vs post-norm in one short paragraph (most modern models normalise *before* each sub-layer; it trains more stably).

### Act IV — How transformers learn, and the three families
**s10 · No peeking: the causal mask, and learning every word at once.** Keep the mask worked example (masked rows **(1, 0, 0)**, **(0.196, 0.804, 0)**, **(0.248, 0.248, 0.503)**; outputs (2, 0), (0.391, 1.609), (1, 1); **w-mask**). New and important: because each position may look only to its left, **one forward pass trains every next-word guess in the sentence at once** — an n-word sentence gives n − 1 (or n) losses in parallel, where an RNN had to walk word by word. Anchor: the whole class answers at once, each student covering the answers to their right.

**s11 · Three families: BERT, GPT and the encoder–decoder.** New. **Encoder-only (BERT, 2018)**: no mask, every word sees both sides; trained by **masked-word guessing** — pick 15% of tokens (of 512 that is **76.8 ≈ 77**), of those 80% become [MASK], 10% a random word, 10% stay (≈ 61.4 / 7.7 / 7.7); good for understanding (classification, search, tagging). **Decoder-only (GPT)**: causal mask, next-word prediction on every position (511 guesses from a 512-token text vs BERT's ~77 — more learning signal per text); good for writing. **Encoder–decoder (the original Transformer, T5)**: encoder reads both ways, decoder writes with a causal mask and **cross-attention** — its queries come from the decoder, keys and values from the encoder: Act I's interpreter, rebuilt from attention alone. Comparison table (what each position may see, training game, typical use, examples). Widget (new): **w-families** — pick a family; the attention-permission grid (who may look at whom) and, under each token, whether it produces a loss; a toggle to run the masking game on a sample sentence.

**s12 · The price: every pair of words.** Keep (n² scores: 1 000 words → **1 000 000** per head per layer; ×12 heads ×12 layers = 144 000 000; the relay's steps must wait in a row; attention's don't). Fix **w-cost** overlapping tick labels. Mention the KV cache as a Unit 19 teaser.

**s13 · A tiny transformer, whole.** Keep the 12-step machine (**w-tiny**; "I drink" → chai **0.877**, loss ≈ **0.45** — re-verify). Add **weight tying**: the final layer that scores every vocabulary word can reuse the embedding table (transposed) — for V = 50 000, d = 512 that saves **25 600 000** weights, and it makes sense: a word's "look-up" vector and its "is-this-the-next-word?" vector should agree.
