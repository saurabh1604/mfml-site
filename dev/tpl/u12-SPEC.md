**Act I — what PCA is looking for**
- **s1 · Pick the better line.** Imagine: photographing a cricket bat — side-on you see its length, end-on it is a dot. Same with a data cloud: some directions show it, some hide it. Companion §1 (two candidate lines, same data; spread 5.45 vs 0.41; "you have just done PCA by eye"). **w-line** (2-D, cinematic): a correlated cloud; drag a line's angle (or its tip); every point drops along a grey thread onto it; coloured dots on the line; live spread meter; below it the spread-vs-angle hump for 0°–180° with a moving marker; peak marked λ₁ and the dip λ₂ exactly 90° away; "▶ sweep" button. Ends with the §1 roadmap list (numbered, like Unit 3).
- **s2 · Why spread is the information.** Companion §2: every model is y = f(x) + ε; a change in y must come from a change in x; variance measures change; PCA never looks at y. Salary table: age, experience, certifications, trainings with variances 64, 41, 0.09, 0.04 (companion Fig 2). Three names for one operation (dimension reduction / feature selection / feature engineering) as a small table. **w-change**: bar chart of the four columns' variances (log toggle) + the age-vs-experience scatter showing they repeat each other, with a button "combine the twins" that draws the one new direction carrying both. Check: a column that is the same for every employee — how much can it explain? (none).
- **s3 · Centre first, then build C.** Imagine: measuring every student's height from the floor vs from the class average — only the second tells you who is tall. Tiny example: the five points (1,2),(4,5),(7,8),(8,4),(10,6), mean (6,5), centred matrix, C = (1/N)XᵀX = [[10,4],[4,4]] **divide by N (=5), not N−1** — this course divides by N. Diagonal = variances, off-diagonal = "move together". **w-cov**: drag points on a 2-D plane; the mean cross moves; live centred table; live C with entries colour-coded (each cell lights the pair of columns it compares); the covariance ellipse drawn. Checks: N vs N−1 for this course; what happens if you forget to centre (raw second moments, different matrix).
- **s4 · Every direction gets one score: wᵀCw.** The three-line derivation Var(z) = (1/N) zᵀz = wᵀCw — data vanishes into C. **w-bowl (3-D)**: the surface q(w) = wᵀCw over the (w₁,w₂) plane (an upward bowl/ellipse-bowl since C is PD), with the unit circle drawn as a glowing ring lying ON the surface; a marker runs along the ring; highest point of the ring = PC1 (orange), lowest = PC2 (green); orbit. Readout: angle, wᵀCw. Realization: "the spread along w is wᵀCw — one number per direction, no data needed." Drawer: the derivation line by line.

**Act II — the maths lands on eigenvectors**
- **s5 · The leash: why length must be 1, and where eigenvectors come from.** Imagine: stretching w doubles every score, quadruples the variance — cheating; so put the direction on a leash of length 1 (w ᵀw = 1). The Lagrangian L = wᵀCw − λ(wᵀw − 1) → 2Cw − 2λw = 0 → Cw = λw. Geometric reading (slides 13–14): at the best point, moving along the circle cannot change f, so ∇f ∥ ∇g. Link back to Unit 11's wall/fine. **w-leash**: 2-D contour map of wᵀCw with the unit circle; drag a point along the circle; show ∇f (orange arrow) and ∇g = 2w (grey arrow); a "parallel meter" (angle between them) that hits 0° exactly at the eigenvectors; tabs for three different C's. Checks. Drawer: Lagrangian derivation + the "why does it work" argument.
- **s6 · The multiplier is the variance.** Substitute back: wᵀCw = λ. Companion's three readings table: price of the constraint / stretch factor / spread along w. **w-three**: pick C (presets incl. [[5,4],[4,5]], [[10,4],[4,4]], [[5,2],[2,8]]); three synchronised panels — (1) Cw vs w arrows (stretch by λ), (2) projected dots with spread readout, (3) the multiplier in the Lagrangian — all showing the same number. Checks: max variance for [[5,4],[4,5]] is 9; an unnormalised eigenvector (1,2) of [[5,2],[2,8]] gives variance 45 = 5 × 9 (so "Var = λ" needs a unit vector).
- **s7 · The second direction, and all the rest.** Two routes, compare them: (a) add "perpendicular to w₁" as a second leash → μ = 0 → Cw₂ = λw₂ (companion §4.5); (b) the slides' deflation: subtract what PC1 already explains, X̂ = X − b₁b₁ᵀX, and find the top direction of what is left; Ŝ has the same eigenvectors, with the used ones sent to eigenvalue 0 (slides 15–21). Spectral theorem gives perpendicularity for free (Unit 4). **w-peel (3-D)**: the 3-D cloud; "find PC1" locks the orange rod; "peel it off" squashes the cloud along PC1 onto the perpendicular plane (animated), the eigenvalue bars update (λ₁ → 0, others unchanged); "find PC2" in the flattened cloud; peel again → PC3. Drawer: the Ŝ eigenvector proof (both cases).
- **s8 · The spread budget is fixed.** tr(C) = Σλ; rotating axes neither creates nor destroys spread; share explained; scree plot; three ways to choose M (target share, elbow, Kaiser λ > 1 on standardised data) — compare them. Warning: a high % can mean one column has huge units. **w-scree**: presets (the companion's five eigenvalues 4.5, 2.1, 0.9, 0.4, 0.1; the singular values 20,10,5,1 with N=100 → λ = 4,1,0.25,0.01; a "noisy tail" set; a standardised 6-feature set for Kaiser); bar scree (log toggle) + running-total curve + a target-% slider that highlights the chosen M; each rule's M shown side by side. Checks: 4.5/2.1/0.9/0.4/0.1 → total 8.0, first two 82.5%, M = 3 for 90% (93.75%).

**Act III — the same answer, seen three more ways**
- **s9 · The second view: the smallest rebuild error.** Encode z = Wᵀx, decode x̃ = Wz = WWᵀx. Error per point = ‖x‖² − (xᵀw)² (Pythagoras), so min total error ⇔ max kept spread. The mean squared error = the eigenvalues you dropped. Worked: new point (12, 9) with the s3 data → centred (6,4), z = 16/√5 ≈ 7.155, x̃ = (12.4, 8.2), error 0.80 = z₂². **w-rebuild**: 2-D cloud and a draggable line; red error threads; a two-part stacked bar "kept + lost = total (fixed)" that trades as you rotate — a seesaw; the lost part hits its minimum exactly where kept hits its max. WWᵀ vs WᵀW explained as the "flattener". Checks. Drawer: the error expansion.
- **s10 · New columns that never repeat each other.** Cov(Z) = WᵀCW = Λ — diagonal → uncorrelated scores; loadings = the recipe weights; worked loadings (6,3,2)/7 and (−1,2,0)/√5 — unit length and perpendicular (the two checks any set of loadings must pass). **w-decor**: before/after scatter (correlation 0.78 → 0.00) with an animated rotation between them; loadings bar chart for PC1/PC2 with feature names (age, experience, certifications); sign-flip button (signs are arbitrary — the pattern matters). Check.
- **s11 · Turn, stretch, turn: the SVD route.** Unit 5's SVD returns: X = UΣVᵀ; C = V(Σ²/N)Vᵀ ⇒ W = V, λ = σ²/N (in the slides' column layout, U). Why squared, why ÷N. Eckart–Young: truncating the SVD is the best rank-M version (packing a suitcase: biggest items first, and no clever repacking beats it); error = dropped singular values (spectral σ_{M+1}, Frobenius √(σ²_{M+1}+…)). **w-eckart**: a procedurally drawn greyscale picture (a simple scene — sun, hills, a tree; NOT a real photo, NOT any known artwork) as a matrix; rank slider 1…full; rebuilt picture, singular-value bars with the kept ones lit, error readout both norms; plus a "data cloud" tab showing a 3-D cloud rebuilt at rank 1/2/3. Checks: σ = (20,10,5,1), N = 100 → λ = 4, 1, 0.25, 0.01; best rank-2 error 5 (spectral), √26 ≈ 5.10 (Frobenius).

**Act IV — PCA in the real world**
- **s12 · Finding the top direction by just multiplying.** Characteristic polynomial works by hand to 4×4; Abel–Ruffini: no formula from degree 5 (not "not known" — impossible), so software iterates, and computing all D eigenvectors to keep 2 is wasteful. Power iteration x_{k+1} = Cx_k/‖Cx_k‖; why it works (eigen-expansion, (λ₂/λ₁)^k); it crawls when λ₂ ≈ λ₁ (and then "which is first" barely matters). **w-power**: arrow swinging towards w₁ pass by pass on the ellipse of C, faint trail; right panel log-angle-to-w₁ vs pass (straight line of slope log(λ₂/λ₁)); sliders for λ₂/λ₁ and start angle; step / play. Check: C = [[5,4],[4,5]] from x₀ = (1,0): x₁ = (5,4)/√41 ≈ (0.781, 0.625) (verify), limit (1,1)/√2; ratio 1/9.
- **s13 · When columns outnumber rows — the Gram trick.** Long vs wide data (40 customers × 12 000 genes). C is D×D regardless of N: 12 000² × 8 bytes = 1.152 GB, ~D³ work. Tempting wrong fix (XXᵀ is not the covariance) → the trick: multiply the eigen-equation by X → K = (1/N)XXᵀ (N×N) has the same non-zero λ; recover w = Xᵀu/‖Xᵀu‖ (don't forget to normalise); rank ≤ N−1 so nothing is lost. **w-wide**: sliders N and D → memory of C vs K on a log bar chart with a "16 GB laptop" line; plus a tiny live numerical demo (N = 3, D = 5 random centred data, re-roll button) listing the non-zero eigenvalues of C (5×5) and K (3×3) side by side — identical — and the recovered w vs the direct w. Checks: 12 000 genes, N = 40 → C is 1.152 GB, K is 40×40, at most 39 non-zero eigenvalues. Drawer: both directions of the proof.
- **s14 · The recipe, and the traps.** Four steps (centre, standardise, eigendecompose, project) + optional undo (x = x̃σ + μ); a new point uses the TRAINING μ, σ, W; the unit trap (height in m vs weight in kg: PC1 loading 1.00 on weight, 0.01 on height before standardising, 0.71/0.71 after); PCA is blind to y (companion Fig 14: the dropped PC2 is the one that predicts y — lighting in images, batch effects); outliers pull components; signs arbitrary; PCA is linear only. **w-recipe**: step-through stage (a)→(f) on one cloud with animated transitions between steps; a "standardise: on/off" switch showing PC1 flip; a "colour by y" tab showing the blind-to-y trap. Checks: Question-3 style — training μ = (4,10), σ = (2,5), α₁ = (3/5,4/5): new x* = (6,20) → standardised (1,2), z = 2.2, x̃ = (1.32, 1.76), error 0.16 = z₂² (z₂ = 0.4).
- **s15 · One example, start to finish.** The s3 five points done completely: mean, centred X (columns sum to 0), C = [[10,4],[4,4]], λ² − 14λ + 24 = 0 → 12, 2 (checks: sum = trace 14, product = det 24), w₁ = (2,1)/√5, w₂ = (1,−2)/√5, scores −13/√5, −4/√5, 5/√5, 3/√5, 9/√5 (≈ −5.814, −1.789, 2.236, 1.342, 4.025), variance of scores = 12, shares 85.71% / 14.29%. **w-worked**: an interactive "PCA calculator": preset datasets (this one; the four points (2,3),(4,7),(6,5),(8,9); the slides' nine-point example (x₁ = 1.11…1.95, x₂ = 10…20) — λ₁ ≈ 10.103, b₁ ≈ (0.0883, 0.9961)) or drag/type your own 2-D points; it shows every step as a card with the live numbers and the checks (column sums 0, Σλ = tr, Πλ = det, unit length, orthogonality, Var(scores) = λ₁), and the picture of points dropping onto PC1.
- **s16 · What to carry forward.** Short summary + the one-page card as a compact table (the problem; the six-line derivation; the five facts; the recipe; the six traps; when things get big) + a paragraph linking to Unit 13 (SVM also turns a geometric wish into a constrained optimisation, and the dual touches data only through dot products — a Gram matrix again).
- **spractice · Practice arena.** 12 problems, each solved in full in the unit's existing practice-arena format (copy Unit 11's markup: problem, what it tests, how to attack it, steps, answer line). Use the companion's seven (Q1 raw data → components [(2,3),(4,7),(6,5),(8,9) → Σ=[[5,4],[4,5]], λ = 9, 1, 90%]; Q2 find the three faults; Q3 new observation; Q4 block-diagonal 3×3 [[4,2,0],[2,4,0],[0,0,3]] → 6, 3, 2; Q5 D ≫ N; Q6 variance shares + Cov(Z) = Λ; Q7 singular values → components + Eckart–Young), plus five new ones with variety in shape (each objective with a definite numeric answer): (P8) the slides' nine-point dataset — given S, find λ₁, b₁, first three scores; (P9) two power-iteration steps on a 2×2; (P10) recover the dropped eigenvalue from the reconstruction error (reverse question); (P11) loadings audit — which of three proposed "component pairs" are valid (unit + orthogonal) with numbers; (P12) Gram-trick numeric: small 2×3 centred X → K, its eigenvalues, recovered w. **Verify every number in `verify-math12.py` (numpy).** Solutions immediately after each problem.
