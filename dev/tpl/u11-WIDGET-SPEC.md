Tabs (`#ar-tabs`): **straight fence** — \(f=x^2+y^2\), fence \(x+y=4\); answer (2, 2), \(\nabla f=(4,4)\), \(\nabla h=(1,1)\), stretch **λ = 4**, \(f^*=8\). **round fence** — \(f=(x-2)^2+y^2\), fence \(x^2+y^2\le 1\); answer (1, 0), \(\nabla f=(-2,0)\), \(\nabla g=(2,0)\), stretch **µ = 1**, \(f^*=1\). **budget circle (ridge)** — grey rings of a training error centred at (2.4, 1.2), the budget \(\beta_1^2+\beta_2^2\le 1\); answer is the ring that just touches the circle — compute it exactly (the touching point is \(\frac{(2.4,1.2)}{\|(2.4,1.2)\|}=(0.894427,0.447214)\)) and label the two coefficients; this is the picture of ridge regression.
Readout: `angle between ∇f and ∇g: <b>…°</b>` (180° at the answer on the round/budget tabs, 0° on the straight-fence tab with the sign convention stated) · `along-the-fence part of ∇f: <b>…</b>` · `stretch factor: <b>…</b>`. Verdict flips to `good — nothing left to slide: all of ∇f points across the fence` when the along-part < 0.02, else `info — there is still <b>…</b> of downhill running ALONG the fence: keep sliding <b>that way →</b>` and draw an s3 arrow showing which way to drag.
`▶ ar-play` walks the point from a bad start to the answer, the along-bar shrinking to nothing.

## W9 · `#w-fine` — turn the wall into a fine  (SVG)  [§9]
**The best widget in the unit. Two panels, both essential.**
(i) **The wall and the line.** Horizontal axis \(g\in[-2,2]\). Draw the indicator \(\mathbb 1(g)\) — flat at 0 for \(g\le0\), a vertical s2 wall shooting off the top at \(g=0\) — and, over it, the straight line \(\mu g\) through the origin (s3). Slider `fn-mu` µ ∈ [−2, 12] step 0.1 (default 2). As µ rises the line tilts up and hugs the wall on the right while staying at or below 0 on the left. Readout: `µ = …  ·  line at g = +0.5: <b>…</b>  vs  wall: <b>∞</b>` and `max over µ ≥ 0 of µg  =  <b>0</b> if g ≤ 0,  <b>∞</b> if g > 0  — the wall, rebuilt out of straight lines`. When µ is dragged **negative** the line dips below zero on the banned side: draw a walker sprinting to the right off the panel and set the verdict `bad — a negative fine PAYS you to trespass. That is why µ ≥ 0.` (This is the whole of dual feasibility, in one drag.)
(ii) **The bill.** The 1-D problem \(f(x)=(x-3)^2\), fence \(x\le1\) (\(g=x-1\)). Draw \(f\) (s1), the true bill \(J(x)=f+\mathbb 1(g)\) (s2, with the vertical cliff at x = 1), and the Lagrangian \(L(x,\mu)=f+\mu g\) (s3) for the current µ. Mark \(\min_x L\) with a dot. Readout: `min over x of L = d(µ) = <b>2µ − µ²/4</b> = …` and `f* on the allowed side = <b>4</b>`. As µ → 4 the dot climbs to exactly 4 and the verdict turns `good — at µ = 4 the straight line does the wall's whole job: d(4) = 4 = p*`. Pushing µ past 4 drops \(d\) again — the concavity you will meet in §13.
Controls: `fn-mu`, `▶ fn-play` (sweeps µ 0 → 8 → 0 over 5 s), presets `fn-p-zero` (0), `fn-p-star` (4), `fn-p-neg` (−1).

## W10 · `#w-room` — room left, and the rule that falls out of it  (SVG)  [§10]
Panel (i): a horizontal **room gauge**. A bar from the point to the fence whose length is \(-g = t^2\), with the fence at 0 and the banned side hatched. Slider `rm-x` moves the answer from deep inside (room 2.0) to hard against the fence (room 0) and just past it (room −0.4, hatched, verdict `bad — infeasible`).
Panel (ii): the **two worlds square** — axes "room left \(-g\)" and "fine rate µ", both ≥ 0. The whole open quadrant is greyed with a big `never lives here` label; only the two edges are live: the µ-axis (fence touched, fine free to be positive) and the room axis (room to spare, fine pinned to 0). The current state is a dot that slides along the two edges as `rm-x` moves, never entering the middle. Readout: `−g = <b>…</b> · µ = <b>…</b> · µ·g = <b>0.000</b>` — the product must print as exactly 0 in every legal state, and the widget should say so: `whatever you drag, this product stays 0`.
Panel (iii): a small **slack ledger** showing \(g + t^2 = 0\) with \(t\) drawn as a physical length and \(t^2\) as its square, so "square = never negative" is visible, not asserted.
Tabs (`#rm-tabs`): **one fence** | **two fences** (two gauges and two dots; the square becomes two squares, and the readout lists the four \((µ_i, g_i)\) patterns with the live one lit).
Verdict strings: inside → `good — room to spare (g < 0), so the fine is forced to zero: this fence is doing no work`; touching → `good — pressed against the fence (g = 0), so the fine is free to be positive: THIS fence is holding you back`; outside → `bad — infeasible: you are past the fence, and no fine rate can rescue it`.

## W11 · `#w-cases` — five conditions, a branching interrogation  (SVG)  [§11]
Two tabs (`#cs-tabs`): **two fences** | **one fence**, plus a third tab **how many?**.

**two fences.** The companion's problem: minimise \(x^2+2y^2\) subject to \(x+y=1\), \(x\ge0\), \(y\ge0\). Left: level curves + the allowed segment (thick s3 between (0,1) and (1,0)) + the four candidate points. Right: a **case tree** — four clickable branches labelled `µ₁=0, µ₂=0`, `µ₁=0, y=0`, `x=0, µ₂=0`, `x=0, y=0`. Clicking a branch solves it live, one line per algebra step in `#cs-work`, and ends in a verdict:
- branch 1 → `x = 2/3, y = 1/3, λ = −4/3, µ₁ = µ₂ = 0, f = 2/3` → `good — survives every condition`
- branch 2 → `y = 0 ⇒ x = 1, λ = −2, µ₂ = −2` → `bad — rejected: µ₂ = −2 < 0 breaks dual feasibility`
- branch 3 → `x = 0 ⇒ y = 1, λ = −4, µ₁ = −4` → `bad — rejected: µ₁ = −4 < 0`
- branch 4 → `x = y = 0 ⇒ x + y = 0 ≠ 1` → `bad — rejected on the path, before any fine rate is computed`
The surviving branch lights its point on the map. A chip prints `λ* = −4/3 is negative — and that is allowed. Only fence multipliers must be ≥ 0.`

**one fence.** The end-to-end problem: minimise \(x^2+y^2\) subject to \(x+y=4\), \(x\ge3\). Two branches: `µ=0` → (2, 2), but \(g=3-2=1>0\) → `bad — rejected: the answer breaks the fence`; `g=0` → \(x=3,y=1,\lambda=-2,\mu=4,p^*=10\) → `good`. Show the five conditions as a checklist `#cs-check` with five ticks appearing one by one (stationarity · path · fence · complementary slackness · µ ≥ 0).

**how many?** A slider `cs-m` m ∈ [1, 8] drawing a binary tree of \(2^m\) leaves with the count printed, beside the wrong guess \(m^2\): a table where the two agree at m = 2 and m = 4 and disagree at **m = 3 (8, not 9)**. Readout `m = 3 → <b>8</b> branches (m² would say 9 — the classic slip)` and `m = 10 → <b>1024</b>; an SVM has one fence per training point, which is why nobody lists cases`.

## W12 · `#w-bowl` — which problems are honest  (SVG)  [§12]
Tabs (`#bw-tabs`): **the chord test** | **the tangent test** | **convex sets**.
- **chord test.** A curve picker (`bw-f`: \(x^2\) · \(x^4-3x^2\) · \(|x|\) · \(e^x\) · \(x^3\) · \(\sqrt{|x|}\)) and two draggable points on it with the chord drawn between them; the region where the chord dips below the curve is flooded var(--critical). Readout: `θ = <b>…</b>  ·  f(θx+(1−θ)y) = <b>…</b>  ·  θf(x)+(1−θ)f(y) = <b>…</b>  ·  <b>≤ holds / FAILS</b>`. A `▶ bw-play` sweeps the two points across the domain and reports `the chord test failed at <b>…</b> of 200 sampled pairs`— 0 for the convex ones.
- **tangent test.** The same curve with a draggable tangent point; the tangent line drawn long; shading wherever the line rises above the curve. Readout: `f(y) ≥ f(x) + f′(x)(y−x)?  <b>holds everywhere / fails on …</b>`. Make the two tests visibly agree on every curve — that equivalence is the section's punchline.
- **convex sets.** Three shapes (disc, half-plane intersection / polygon, an annulus and a crescent) with two draggable points and the segment between them; the segment turns var(--critical) where it leaves the set. Readout `the segment leaves the set: <b>yes/no</b>`.
A permanent chip across all tabs: `convex ⇒ a KKT point is THE answer. Not convex ⇒ a KKT point is only a candidate.`

## W13 · `#w-duel` — who moves first  (SVG)  [§13]
Tabs (`#du-tabs`): **two curves** | **a bundle of lines** | **the gap**.
- **two curves.** Side by side: left, the primal \(f(x)=(x-3)^2\) in x with the allowed strip \(x\le1\) shaded and \(p^*=4\) marked at \(x=1\); right, the dual \(d(\mu)=2\mu-\mu^2/4\) in µ ≥ 0 with \(d^*=4\) at \(\mu^*=4\). Sliders `du-x` and `du-mu` move a dot on each. A gap chip: `p(x) = <b>…</b>   d(µ) = <b>…</b>   gap so far = <b>…</b>` and, at the two optima, `p* = 4 = d*  →  zero duality gap (strong duality)`. Draw a dashed horizontal line at 4 spanning both panels: it is the same number seen from two sides.
- **a bundle of lines.** THE picture. Axis µ ∈ [0, 8]. For each of ~40 fixed values of x, draw the straight line \(\mu\mapsto L(x,\mu)=f(x)+\mu g(x)\) (height \(f(x)\), slope \(g(x)\)) in faint s1. Their **lower envelope**, drawn thick in s3, is \(d(\mu)\) — visibly concave, a bundle of straight lines bending downward. A slider `du-xl` highlights one x and its line in s4 with a chip `x = … → height f(x) = …, slope g(x) = …`. Readout: `d is the lowest of 40 straight lines. The lowest of any bundle of straight lines bends downward — so the dual is ALWAYS concave, however ugly f is.`
- **the gap.** A non-convex problem where the gap is real: minimise \(f(x)=x\) subject to \(g(x)=(x^2-1)(x^2-4)\le0\), whose feasible set is the two intervals \([-2,-1]\cup[1,2]\), so \(p^*=-2\). Draw \(g\) with the two allowed bands shaded, and beside it \(d(\mu)=\min_x\,[\,x+\mu(x^4-5x^2+4)\,]\) computed numerically on a fine grid of x for each µ (and by solving \(1+\mu(4x^3-10x)=0\) — a grid of 4000 points over \(x\in[-6,6]\) is fine and fast). Mark \(d^*=\max_{\mu\ge0}d(\mu)\) and print `p* = <b>−2</b>  ·  d* = <b>…</b>  ·  duality gap = <b>…</b> > 0`. **Compute d\* yourself and put the number in your report** — the verify script will assert it. Verdict `info — weak duality still holds (d* ≤ p*); it is strong duality that needs convexity`.

## W14 · `#w-price` — what a wall is worth  (SVG)  [§13]
The shadow price, drawn as a race between two numbers. Problem: minimise \(x^2+y^2\) subject to \(x+y=4\) and \(x\ge c\). Slider `pr-c` c ∈ [2, 3.6] step 0.01 (default 3.00).
Panel (i): the level curves + the line \(x+y=4\) with the allowed part in s3 and the answer dot moving as c moves.
Panel (ii): the **value curve** \(p^*(c)\) plotted against c (exact: \(p^*=8\) for \(c\le2\), and \(c^2+(4-c)^2\) for \(c>2\)), with the s3 tangent at c = 3 whose slope is \(\mu^*=4\) — actually \(\mathrm dp^*/\mathrm dc = 2c-2(4-c)=4c-8=4\) at \(c=3\). Readouts: `c = 3.00 → x* = 3, y* = 1, µ* = <b>4</b>, p* = <b>10</b>` and, as c moves, `predicted by the price:  10 + 4·Δc = <b>…</b>   ·   actually: <b>…</b>   ·   error <b>…</b>`. At c = 2.9 the two read **9.60** and **9.62**. Verdict `good — the multiplier told you the change before you re-solved anything`.
`.note`: at c ≤ 2 the fence stops biting, µ falls to 0, and the value curve goes flat — the price of a wall you are not touching is nothing.

## W15 · `#w-svm` — two points and a line  (SVG)  [§14]
One dimension, two labelled points: \(x_1=+1\) (class +1, s3) and \(x_2=-1\) (class −1, s2) on a horizontal axis, with the decision point, the margin band and the two margin edges drawn.
Controls: tabs `sv-tabs` **the answer** | **solve the dual** | **add a point**.
- **the answer.** Static-but-live: the boundary at \(x=0\), edges at \(x=\pm1\), margin width \(2/|w|=\mathbf 2\), with `w* = 1, b* = 0, α₁ = α₂ = 0.5`. A slider `sv-w` lets the student move \(w\) (with \(b\) fixed at 0) and watch the margin width \(2/|w|\) grow while the constraints \(y_i(wx_i+b)\ge1\) go red as soon as \(w<1\): readout `margin = <b>…</b>  ·  constraints: <b>satisfied / violated</b>  ·  ½w² = <b>…</b>`. The verdict is `good — w = 1 is the smallest w that still clears both constraints: widest margin = smallest ½w²`.
- **solve the dual.** The curve \(q(\alpha)=2\alpha-2\alpha^2\) over α ≥ 0 with a slider `sv-a`; the peak at \(\alpha^*=\tfrac12\), \(d^*=\tfrac12\); a side line showing \(w=\alpha_1+\alpha_2=2\alpha\) tracking the slider so the student sees the classifier being *built out of the multipliers*. Readout `α = … → w = … → ½w² = … · q(α) = …` and at the peak `α* = 0.5, w* = 1, d* = 0.5 = p*`.
- **add a point.** A third point, draggable along the axis (`sv-x3` ∈ [1, 4], default 3, class +1). While it sits outside the margin its multiplier is **0**, the classifier does not move, and the point is drawn hollow with the label `α₃ = 0 — delete it and nothing changes`. Drag it inside \(x_3<1\) and the problem changes: the classifier shifts and the point fills in as a support vector. Readout `α₃ = <b>…</b> · support vectors: <b>…</b> of 3 · boundary x = <b>…</b> · margin = <b>…</b>`. **Compute the shifted solution exactly** for \(x_3<1\) (hard margin with points at +1 and x₃ both class +1, and −1 class −1: the binding pair is \(x_3\) and −1, giving \(w=2/(x_3+1)\), \(b=-w\,(x_3-1)/2\)… derive it, print it, and put the formula in your report). Verdict `good — complementary slackness is why an SVM is sparse: only the points whose constraint is tight carry the answer`.
`.note`: "Every point with \(\alpha_i=0\) could be deleted from the training set and the classifier would not move. That one sentence is where Unit 14 begins."
