## Hero · "the canyon becomes a bowl" (`#hero-3d`)
The thesis in one shot. Verify both Hessians in code (H = 2 Zᵀ Z) and print them once to the console at load (`console.info`, not error).
Draw the surface over a window centred on the minimiser `w*(t)` (solve the normal equations for each t) with half-widths chosen so the picture stays readable: at t=0 use `w₁ ∈ w₁* ± 2.5`, `w₂ ∈ w₂* ± 0.10` — i.e. the domain is itself anisotropic, and you must map the domain to a *square* stage footprint (`P=(x,y,z)=>[(x−cx)/hx·2.2, z*ZS, −(y−cy)/hy·2.2]`) so the razor blade appears as a long narrow trench; at t=1 half-widths `± 3.5` both. ## W11 · `#w-clinic` — the training clinic (SVG `#cl-svg` 600×340 + a diagnosis panel `#cl-panel`)
Six recorded training runs the student must diagnose — the lecture's "toolkit" as a game. Buttons `cl-cases [data-c]` (case 1..6; case 1 default) each showing a **loss-vs-step chart** (s4 glowing curve on a log-scale y where finite) plus a small `train / test` pair where relevant (s3 train, s2 test):
1. `NaN` — loss 3.2 → 8e5 → 2e11 → ∞ → NaN in 5 steps (draw the ∞/NaN steps as critical hatched chips above the chart). Diagnosis: `overflow: huge start or unscaled features`; fix: `initialise in [−1, 1], scale the features, lower η`.
2. `memoriser` — train loss falls smoothly to 0.00 by step 60; test loss falls to 0.9 at step 25 then RISES to 3.1. Diagnosis: `overfitting: more knobs than facts`; fix: `more data, fewer knobs, regularise, stop early`.
3. `oscillates` — loss 12, 4, 11, 5, 13, 6… (a sawtooth that slowly grows). Diagnosis: `η above 2/λ_max in the stiff direction`; fix: `reduce η or standardise the features`.
4. `fast then crawls` — loss drops from 40 to 3 in 8 steps, then 3 → 2.6 over the next 200. Diagnosis: `zig-zag in a badly conditioned canyon`; fix: `standardise; momentum / adaptive steps (next unit)`.
5. `flat from the start` — loss 6.00, 5.99, 5.99, 5.98 … for 200 steps. Diagnosis: `a plateau, or η far too small`; fix: `raise η, better start, check the gradient code`.
6. `different every time` — three runs from three seeds ending at 0.4, 2.1, 0.4 (draw the three curves s4/s7/s2). Diagnosis: `several local minima, different basins`; fix: `restarts, keep the best — or accept it`.
Each case: the chart, then `#cl-panel` offers **four diagnosis buttons** (the right one + three wrong ones drawn from the other cases' diagnoses; deterministic order per case — put the correct one at a different position each time: positions [2,4,1,3,2,4]). Click → the panel reveals the verdict (good `✓ …diagnosis… — fix: …` / bad `✗ not this one: …why not, one clause… — try again`) and, on a right answer, the chart gets a s3 "after the fix" ghost curve overlaid (a plausible healthy curve). A score chip `… of 6 diagnosed` (in-memory only, not stored). Readout below the chart: the run's raw numbers as a compact line (`loss: 3.2 → 8e5 → 2e11 → ∞ → NaN`). All data hard-coded arrays; no randomness.
