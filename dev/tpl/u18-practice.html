Practice arena — fourteen problems, solved in full

Fourteen problems, easy to hard: one lookup by hand, a whole attention table, the \(\sqrt d\) scaling and the spread, the causal mask, head shapes and weight counts, rotary and clock positions, layer norm, a block's weights, the \(n^2\) bill, a residual step, a reverse-engineering puzzle and a spiky row to diagnose. Every number here was checked by machine.

Three habits do most of the work. Write the shapes first: \(QK^{\mathsf T}\) is words × words, and the answer has one row per word. Work row by row: each row of the attention map is its own little softmax. And check that each row of shares adds up to 1 before you blend the values.

Problem 1easyone lookup

Queries and keys have length \(d=4\). The query is \(\mathbf q=(2,0,0,0)\). Two words have keys \(\mathbf k_1=(1,0,0,0)\), \(\mathbf k_2=(0,1,0,0)\) and values \(\mathbf v_1=(4,2)\), \(\mathbf v_2=(0,6)\). Find (a) the two scaled scores, (b) the two shares, (c) the answer.

What this tests. The four moves of §3: dot product, divide by \(\sqrt d\), softmax, blend. Plan. \(\sqrt4=2\); then one softmax of two numbers.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — scores. \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k_1=2\), \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k_2=0\). Divide by \(\sqrt4=2\): \((1,\ 0)\).
Step 2 — shares. \(e^1\approx2.718\), \(e^0=1\), total \(3.718\): \((0.731,\ 0.269)\).
Step 3 — blend. \(0.731\,(4,2)+0.269\,(0,6)=(2.924,\ 1.462+1.613)\approx(2.924,\ 3.076)\).

answers at a glance: (a) \((1,0)\). (b) \((0.731,\ 0.269)\). (c) \(\approx(2.924,\ 3.076)\).

Remember

The keys decide the shares; the values decide what is blended. They can even have different lengths.

Problem 2easythe whole table

Three words have vectors \(\mathbf x_1=(1,0)\), \(\mathbf x_2=(0,1)\), \(\mathbf x_3=(1,1)\), and each word uses its own vector as query, key and value (\(Q=K=V=X\), \(d=2\)). Find (a) the scaled score table \(QK^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2\), (b) the attention matrix, (c) the three answers.

What this tests. \(\operatorname{softmax}(QK^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt d)V\) row by row. Plan. All dot products are 0, 1 or 2; divide by 1.414; softmax each row.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — dot products. \(QK^{\mathsf T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\ 0&1&1\\ 1&1&2\end{pmatrix}\). Divide by \(\sqrt2\): entries \(0.707\) for 1 and \(1.414\) for 2.
Step 2 — row 1. Scores \((0.707,0,0.707)\): \(e^{0.707}\approx2.028\), total \(5.056\): shares \((0.401,\ 0.198,\ 0.401)\). Row 2 is the mirror: \((0.198,\ 0.401,\ 0.401)\).
Step 3 — row 3. Scores \((0.707,0.707,1.414)\): \(2.028+2.028+4.113=8.169\): shares \((0.248,\ 0.248,\ 0.503)\).
Step 4 — answers. Row 1: \(0.401(1,0)+0.198(0,1)+0.401(1,1)=(0.802,\ 0.599)\). Row 2: \((0.599,\ 0.802)\). Row 3: \(0.248(1,0)+0.248(0,1)+0.503(1,1)=(0.752,\ 0.752)\).

answers at a glance: (a) \(\begin{pmatrix}0.707&0&0.707\\ 0&0.707&0.707\\ 0.707&0.707&1.414\end{pmatrix}\). (b) rows \((0.401,0.198,0.401)\), \((0.198,0.401,0.401)\), \((0.248,0.248,0.503)\). (c) \((0.802,0.599)\), \((0.599,0.802)\), \((0.752,0.752)\).

Remember

With \(Q=K\) the score table is symmetric, but the attention matrix need not be: each row is normalised on its own.

Problem 3easy√d scaling

Two raw scores are \(8\) and \(0\), with \(d=64\). Find the first share and its slope \(p(1-p)\) (a) without scaling, (b) after dividing by \(\sqrt d\). (c) By roughly what factor did the slope grow?

What this tests. How scaling changes a softmax row and its gradient. Plan. Two-word softmax is \(1/(1+e^{-\text{gap}})\).

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). Gap 8: \(p=1/(1+e^{-8})\approx0.99966\); slope \(0.99966\times0.00034\approx0.000335\).
Step 2 — (b). \(\sqrt{64}=8\), so the gap is 1: \(p=1/(1+e^{-1})\approx0.731\); slope \(0.731\times0.269\approx0.197\).
Step 3 — (c). \(0.197/0.000335\approx586\).

answers at a glance: (a) \(p\approx0.99966\), slope \(\approx0.000335\). (b) \(p\approx0.731\), slope \(\approx0.197\). (c) about 590 times.

Remember

A nearly one-hot row passes almost no blame. Scaling by \(\sqrt d\) keeps rows soft and trainable.

Problem 4easythe spread

Entries of \(\mathbf q\) and \(\mathbf k\) are independent with mean 0 and variance 1. Give the spread (standard deviation) of \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k\) for (a) \(d=16\), (b) \(d=256\), (c) \(d=128\). (d) A model divides its scores by 8 while \(d=256\). What is the spread of its scaled scores?

What this tests. \(\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k)=d\). Plan. Take square roots; dividing by \(c\) divides the spread by \(c\).

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a), (b), (c). \(\sqrt{16}=4\), \(\sqrt{256}=16\), \(\sqrt{128}\approx11.31\).
Step 2 — (d). The raw spread is 16; divided by 8 it is 2. The right divisor would have been \(\sqrt{256}=16\).

answers at a glance: (a) 4. (b) 16. (c) \(\approx11.31\). (d) 2 (it should have divided by 16).

Remember

Spread grows like \(\sqrt d\), so divide by \(\sqrt d\) — not by \(d\), and not by a number picked for another size.

Problem 5mediumcausal mask

A 3-word decoder has scaled scores \(S=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\ 1&3&1\\ 0&2&2\end{pmatrix}\). Apply the causal mask and find (a) the masked score table, (b) the attention matrix.

What this tests. \(-\infty\) above the diagonal, then a row softmax. Plan. Row 1 has one cell, row 2 two cells, row 3 all three.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(\begin{pmatrix}2&-\infty&-\infty\\ 1&3&-\infty\\ 0&2&2\end{pmatrix}\).
Step 2 — row 1. Only one allowed cell: \((1,0,0)\). (The score 2 does not matter.)
Step 3 — row 2. \(e^1\approx2.718\), \(e^3\approx20.086\), total \(22.804\): \((0.119,\ 0.881,\ 0)\).
Step 4 — row 3. \(e^0=1\), \(e^2\approx7.389\) twice, total \(15.778\): \((0.063,\ 0.468,\ 0.468)\).

answers at a glance: (a) as above. (b) rows \((1,0,0)\), \((0.119,0.881,0)\), \((0.063,0.468,0.468)\).

Remember

The first row of a masked map is always \((1,0,\dots,0)\). The last row is never changed by the mask.

Problem 6mediumhead shapes

A model has \(d=768\) and \(h=12\) heads. Find (a) \(d_k\); (b) the shape and weight count of one head's \(W_Q^{(i)}\); (c) the weights of \(W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O\) together; (d) the same with one bias per output entry of each of the four matrices.

What this tests. \(d_k=d/h\) and the \(4d^2\) count. Plan. One head, then all heads, then \(W_O\).

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(d_k=768/12=64\).
Step 2 — (b). \(W_Q^{(i)}\) is \(768\times64\): \(49\,152\) weights.
Step 3 — (c). 12 heads × 3 matrices × 49 152 \(=1\,769\,472=3\times768^2\). Add \(W_O\), \(768^2=589\,824\): total \(2\,359\,296=4\times768^2\).
Step 4 — (d). Four matrices each have 768 outputs: \(+4\times768=3072\), giving \(2\,362\,368\).

answers at a glance: (a) 64. (b) \(768\times64\), 49 152. (c) 2 359 296. (d) 2 362 368.

Remember

More heads means smaller heads. The weight count, \(4d^2\), does not depend on \(h\).

Problem 7mediumrotary positions

RoPE with \(\theta=30^\circ\), \(\mathbf q=(1,0)\), \(\mathbf k=(0,1)\). The score is \((R_{m\theta}\mathbf q)\cdot(R_{n\theta}\mathbf k)\). Find it for (a) \(m=4,n=1\), (b) \(m=7,n=4\), (c) \(m=1,n=4\). (d) Give the general formula in terms of \(m-n\).

What this tests. That the score depends only on the gap. Plan. Turn both arrows, then take the dot product; spot the pattern.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(\mathbf q\) turned by \(120^\circ\): \((\cos120^\circ,\sin120^\circ)=(-0.5,\ 0.866)\). \(\mathbf k=(0,1)\) points at \(90^\circ\); turned by \(30^\circ\) it points at \(120^\circ\) too: \((-0.5,\ 0.866)\). Dot product \(0.25+0.75=1\).
Step 2 — (b). Same gap 3: arrows at \(210^\circ\) and \(90^\circ+120^\circ=210^\circ\). Score 1.
Step 3 — (c). Arrows at \(30^\circ\) and \(90^\circ+120^\circ=210^\circ\): opposite. Score \(-1\).
Step 4 — (d). \(\mathbf q\) points at \(m\theta\), \(\mathbf k\) at \(90^\circ+n\theta\). The dot product of two unit arrows is the cosine of the angle between them: \(\cos(90^\circ+(n-m)\theta)=\sin((m-n)\theta)\).

answers at a glance: (a) 1. (b) 1. (c) −1. (d) \(\sin((m-n)\theta)\).

Remember

Rotary scores depend only on \(m-n\): "3 words back" scores the same anywhere in the sentence.

Problem 8mediumclock tags

Sinusoidal tags: \(\mathrm{PE}(p,2i)=\sin(p/10000^{2i/d})\), \(\mathrm{PE}(p,2i+1)=\cos(p/10000^{2i/d})\). (a) For \(d=4\), write the tag of position \(p=2\). (b) For \(d=512\), what is the speed \(1/10000^{2i/d}\) of clock \(i=128\)? (c) How many positions does that clock need for one full turn?

What this tests. Reading the clock formula. Plan. For \(d=4\) the speeds are \(1\) and \(10000^{-1/2}=0.01\).

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). Clock 0: \(\sin2\approx0.909\), \(\cos2\approx-0.416\). Clock 1: \(\sin0.02\approx0.020\), \(\cos0.02\approx1.000\).
Step 2 — (b). \(2i/d=256/512=\tfrac12\), so the speed is \(10000^{-1/2}=0.01\) radians per position.
Step 3 — (c). One full turn is \(2\pi\) radians: \(2\pi/0.01\approx628\) positions.

answers at a glance: (a) \((0.909,\ -0.416,\ 0.020,\ 1.000)\). (b) 0.01. (c) about 628 positions.

Remember

Fast clocks separate neighbours; slow clocks separate far-apart positions.

Problem 9mediumlayer norm

Layer-normalise \(\mathbf x=(2,4,4,6)\) (a) with \(\boldsymbol\gamma=1,\boldsymbol\beta=0\), (b) with \(\gamma=2,\beta=1\) in every entry. (c) What do you get for \(\mathbf x'=(12,14,14,16)\)?

What this tests. Mean, variance (divide by \(d\)), then scale and shift. Plan. Centre first.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — mean and variance. Mean 4; centred \((-2,0,0,2)\); variance \((4+0+0+4)/4=2\); spread \(\sqrt2\approx1.414\).
Step 2 — (a). \((-2,0,0,2)/1.414=(-1.414,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1.414)\).
Step 3 — (b). \(2\times(\text{a})+1=(-1.828,\ 1,\ 1,\ 3.828)\).
Step 4 — (c). \(\mathbf x'=\mathbf x+10\): the shift disappears when we centre, so the result is the same as (a).

answers at a glance: (a) \((-1.414,0,0,1.414)\). (b) \((-1.828,1,1,3.828)\). (c) \((-1.414,0,0,1.414)\).

Remember

Layer norm forgets the average and the size of a word's numbers; \(\gamma\) and \(\beta\) let the network put back what it needs.

Problem 10mediuma block's weights

A block has \(d=256\) and \(d_{ff}=1024\). Count (a) the attention weights, (b) the feed-forward weights, (c) the total with all biases, (d) the total with biases and both layer norms.

What this tests. \(4d^2+2dd_{ff}\), biases \(4d+d_{ff}+d\), layer norms \(4d\). Plan. Add in stages.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(4\times256^2=262\,144\).
Step 2 — (b). \(2\times256\times1024=524\,288\).
Step 3 — (c). Weights \(786\,432\), biases \(4(256)+1024+256=2304\): \(788\,736\).
Step 4 — (d). Two layer norms: \(2\times(256+256)=1024\): \(789\,760\).

answers at a glance: (a) 262 144. (b) 524 288. (c) 788 736. (d) 789 760.

Remember

With \(d_{ff}=4d\), two thirds of a block's weights are in the feed-forward part.

Problem 11mediumthe n² bill

A model reads \(n=2048\) tokens with 16 heads and 24 layers. (a) How many scores are in one head's map? (b) How many scores in the whole forward pass? (c) At 2 bytes per score, how many gigabytes (\(10^9\) bytes) to keep them all? (d) By what factor does (b) grow at \(n=4096\)?

What this tests. \(n^2\times h\times L\). Plan. Square, multiply, convert.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(2048^2=4\,194\,304\).
Step 2 — (b). \(4\,194\,304\times16\times24=1\,610\,612\,736\).
Step 3 — (c). \(\times2\) bytes \(=3\,221\,225\,472\) bytes \(\approx3.22\) GB.
Step 4 — (d). Doubling \(n\) multiplies \(n^2\) by 4.

answers at a glance: (a) 4 194 304. (b) 1 610 612 736. (c) \(\approx3.22\) GB. (d) 4 times.

Remember

The attention bill grows with the square of the length. That is why long inputs are expensive.

Problem 12mediumresidual + norm

A word has \(\mathbf x=(1,0,-1,0)\) and attention returns \(\mathbf a=(1,2,1,0)\). Find (a) \(\mathbf x+\mathbf a\), (b) its mean and variance, (c) \(\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf x+\mathbf a)\) with \(\gamma=1,\beta=0\). (d) What would \(\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf a)\) alone be (no shortcut)?

What this tests. The add-and-steady step. Plan. Add, centre, divide.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \((2,2,0,0)\).
Step 2 — (b). Mean 1; centred \((1,1,-1,-1)\); variance \(4/4=1\).
Step 3 — (c). \((1,1,-1,-1)/1=(1,\ 1,\ -1,\ -1)\).
Step 4 — (d). \(\mathbf a\) has mean 1, centred \((0,1,0,-1)\), variance \(2/4=0.5\): \((0,1,0,-1)/0.707=(0,\ 1.414,\ 0,\ -1.414)\). Without the shortcut the word's own first entry is lost.

answers at a glance: (a) \((2,2,0,0)\). (b) mean 1, variance 1. (c) \((1,1,-1,-1)\). (d) \((0,1.414,0,-1.414)\).

Remember

The shortcut keeps the word's own information in the sum; layer norm only rescales.

Problem 13hardreverse-engineer

Keys \(\mathbf k_1=(1,0)\), \(\mathbf k_3=(1,1)\) and a query \(\mathbf q=(2,0)\), \(d=2\). Word 2's key is changed to \(\mathbf k_2=(c,0)\). (a) Write the three scaled scores in terms of \(c\). (b) Find \(c\) so that word 2 gets exactly half of all the attention. (c) What are the three shares then? (d) For which \(c\) does word 2 get the biggest share of the three?

What this tests. Running softmax backwards. Plan. "Half" means \(e^{s_2}=e^{s_1}+e^{s_3}\).

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k_1=2\), \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k_2=2c\), \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf k_3=2\). Divided by \(\sqrt2\): \((\sqrt2,\ \sqrt2\,c,\ \sqrt2)\).
Step 2 — (b). Half the total means \(e^{\sqrt2c}=e^{\sqrt2}+e^{\sqrt2}=2e^{\sqrt2}\). Take logs: \(\sqrt2\,c=\sqrt2+\ln2\), so \(c=1+\ln2/\sqrt2\approx1+0.490=1.490\).
Step 3 — (c). Word 2 has \(\tfrac12\); words 1 and 3 have equal scores, so they split the rest: \((0.25,\ 0.5,\ 0.25)\).
Step 4 — (d). Word 2 beats words 1 and 3 exactly when its score is bigger: \(\sqrt2c>\sqrt2\), that is \(c>1\).

answers at a glance: (a) \((1.414,\ 1.414c,\ 1.414)\). (b) \(c=1+\ln2/\sqrt2\approx1.490\). (c) \((0.25,0.5,0.25)\). (d) \(c>1\).

Remember

To win a fixed share, a key needs a fixed gap in log space: shares follow \(e^{\text{score}}\).

Problem 14harddiagnose a run

A student's model with \(d=64\) stops learning. One attention row has raw scores \((40,\ 32,\ 8,\ 0)\), and those are the numbers that go into softmax. (a) What shares does the row get? (b) What is the slope \(p(1-p)\) of the top share? (c) What was forgotten, and what are the shares after the fix? (d) What is the top share's slope after the fix?

What this tests. Spotting missing \(\sqrt d\) scaling from a spiky, frozen row. Plan. Softmax cares only about gaps; the gap 8 is huge.

Show the full solution
Step 1 — (a). Subtract the biggest score: \((0,-8,-32,-40)\). \(e^{-8}\approx0.000335\), the others are tiny: shares \(\approx(0.99966,\ 0.00034,\ 0,\ 0)\).
Step 2 — (b). \(0.99966\times0.00034\approx0.000335\): almost no blame gets through, so the row cannot learn.
Step 3 — (c). The scores were never divided by \(\sqrt{64}=8\). Fixed: \((5,4,1,0)\). \(e^5\approx148.41\), \(e^4\approx54.60\), \(e^1\approx2.72\), \(e^0=1\), total \(206.73\): shares \(\approx(0.718,\ 0.264,\ 0.013,\ 0.005)\).
Step 4 — (d). \(0.718\times0.282\approx0.203\) — about 600 times more than before.

answers at a glance: (a) \(\approx(0.99966,0.00034,0,0)\). (b) \(\approx0.000335\). (c) the \(1/\sqrt d\) scaling; \(\approx(0.718,0.264,0.013,0.005)\). (d) \(\approx0.203\).

Remember

A row that is almost exactly one-hot, with the model not learning, is the classic sign of missing \(\sqrt d\) scaling (or a temperature that is far too low).
