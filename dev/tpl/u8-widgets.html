Term \(n\) has size \(|x|^n/n!\) (times a bounded derivative). A power \(|x|^n\) multiplies by the same \(|x|\) every step; a factorial multiplies by \(n\), which eventually dwarfs any fixed \(|x|\). That race is the whole reason the exponential and trigonometric series converge everywhere — and \(\ln(1+x)\), whose terms shrink only like \(|x|^n/n\), loses the race the moment \(|x|>1\).

Row × matrix × column is a double sum: \(\mathbf h^{\top}H\mathbf h=\sum_i\sum_j h_i\,H_{ij}\,h_j\). Each entry of \(H\) feeds one term, and each term uses the step component of its row and of its column. Because \(H\) is symmetric, the mirror-image entries \(H_{ij}\) and \(H_{ji}\) feed the same product and merge — for two variables that merge is the familiar \(h^2f_{xx}+2hkf_{xy}+k^2f_{yy}\).
