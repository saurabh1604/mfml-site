## Sections (ids stable; 14 + practice). Each: Imagine this → tiny example → widget(s) with "Try:" → The realization → checks → `<!--@ALG sid-->` drawer (where it earns one) → In one sentence.

**Act I — uncertainty has a shape**
- **s1 · Why a machine needs probabilities.** Units 1–13 gave crisp answers; the world gives maybes. An SVM says "+1" with the same voice whether the point is deep inside or grazing the street. A forecaster who says "70% rain" and is right 7 times in 10 is more useful than one who says "rain" every day. A random variable is a rule that turns an uncertain outcome into a number. **w-galton**: 2-D Galton board — drop 1 / 100 / 2000 beads, peg-bias slider, rows slider; the histogram converges to a shape; "the shape is the knowledge". End with the unit's roadmap.
- **s2 · Distributions: where the probability lives.** Discrete: the sum of two dice, pmf 1/36 … 6/36 … 1/36; expectation as a balance point (E = 7), variance as spread (Var = 35/6 ≈ 5.833; one die: E = 3.5, Var = 35/12 ≈ 2.917). Continuous: probability is area, not height — a density can exceed 1 (N(0, 0.1²) peaks at ≈ 3.989); P(X = exactly 5) = 0. **w-dice**: choose 1/2/3 dice; bars on a see-saw that balances at the mean; variance shown as the spread bar; roll-simulation toggle that shows the empirical histogram approaching the pmf. **w-density**: a density curve with a draggable [a, b] window, area readout vs height readout; squeeze σ to watch the peak climb above 1 while the area stays 1. Checks. Drawer: E and Var of one die; Var = E[X²] − (E X)².
- **s3 · Bayes: turning evidence around.** The clinic puzzle: 1% of people have the disease; the test catches 99% of the sick; it falsely flags 5% of the healthy. You test positive. P(sick | +) = 0.0099 / (0.0099 + 0.0495) = **1/6 ≈ 0.167**. A second independent positive: prior 1/6 → **≈ 0.798**. Read it as counts: 10 000 people, 100 sick, 99 flagged; 9 900 healthy, 495 flagged. Posterior ∝ likelihood × prior; "the prior is where you stood before the evidence". **w-bayes**: a 10 000-person icon grid (natural frequencies) with prevalence / sensitivity / false-positive sliders; the positive group highlighted and the posterior as the fraction of it that is sick; "test again" button chains the update. Checks. Drawer: Bayes from P(A∩B) two ways.

**Act II — the Gaussian**
- **s4 · The bell curve and why it keeps showing up.** Many small independent nudges add up to a bell (the central limit theorem, told as: heights, measurement error, marks). The formula read aloud (a distance from the mean, measured in σ's, squared, then exp of minus half of it, normalised). 68–95–99.7; z-scores. **w-bell**: μ, σ sliders, shade ±1σ/±2σ/±3σ with areas, draggable x with z-score and density readouts. **w-clt**: average of n rolls of a (deliberately lopsided) die, n = 1…30, histogram morphing into a bell with the matching Gaussian overlaid. Checks.
- **s5 · The Gaussian in many dimensions.** Mean vector + covariance matrix; the contour is an ellipse (Unit 12's covariance ellipse — same object); axes = eigenvectors of Σ, axis lengths ∝ √eigenvalues (Unit 4); the quadratic form (x−μ)ᵀΣ⁻¹(x−μ) = Mahalanobis distance ("distance measured in the data's own ruler"). Worked: Σ = [[2,1],[1,2]] → eigenvalues 3 and 1, axes (1,1)/√2 and (1,−1)/√2, det 3, peak density 1/(2π√3) ≈ 0.0919. Independent vs correlated; ρ = 0 means circles/axis-aligned ellipses. **w-gauss2d (3-D, flagship)**: a density mountain over the plane, sliders σ₁, σ₂, ρ (and μ drag); the floor shows contours, the eigen-axes, and a Mahalanobis probe point with both "ordinary distance" and "Mahalanobis distance" readouts; "slice" toggle shows that every vertical slice is itself a 1-D bell. Checks. Drawer: why the contour of a quadratic form is an ellipse with eigen-axes.
- **s6 · Making Gaussian data: stretch a round cloud.** Draw z from the round N(0, I), then x = μ + Lz with LLᵀ = Σ (Cholesky, Unit 5). Worked: Σ = [[4,2],[2,2]] → L = [[2,0],[1,1]]. The same trick is the "reparameterisation" of Unit 20. **w-stretch**: 400 round samples; edit L (or Σ, with L computed live); watch the cloud shear/stretch into the ellipse with the sample covariance readout converging to Σ. Checks. Drawer: Cov(Lz) = L Cov(z) Lᵀ = LLᵀ.

**Act III — learning = choosing the world that makes your data least surprising**
- **s7 · Likelihood: turning the question around.** Probability asks "given the coin, how likely is the data?" Likelihood asks "given the data, which coin?". 7 heads in 10: L(p) = p⁷(1−p)³, peak at p = 0.7 (L ≈ 0.00222 — tiny numbers, which is why we take logs: products → sums, same peak). **w-coin**: toss button / set heads and tosses; the likelihood curve and the log-likelihood curve side by side, both peaking at heads/tosses; more tosses → sharper peak (confidence grows). Checks. Drawer: d/dp log L = 0 ⇒ p̂ = h/n.
- **s8 · Fitting a Gaussian by maximum likelihood.** Data 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 → μ̂ = 5, σ̂² = 4 (divide by N, σ̂ = 2); the n−1 version (≈ 4.571) and why the MLE is slightly too small (it measures spread around its own centre) — the same divide-by-N vs n−1 note as Unit 12. **w-mle (3-D)**: the log-likelihood surface over (μ, σ) with the peak at (5, 2); drag a point on the surface and watch the matching bell over the data on a side panel — too narrow, too wide, off-centre, just right; a ball rolls uphill (gradient ascent, Unit 9) to the peak. Checks. Drawer: both derivatives set to zero.
- **s9 · Least squares falls out.** Assume y = wx + b + Gaussian noise ⇒ −log L = (1/2σ²) Σ (yᵢ − ŷᵢ)² + const ⇒ maximising likelihood = minimising squared error (Unit 3's projection had a probabilistic meaning all along). Worked: x = 0,1,2,3, y = 1,3,2,5 → w = 1.1, b = 1.1, SSE = 2.70. Variant: Laplace noise ⇒ absolute error ⇒ a fit that shrugs off outliers. **w-lsq (3-D)**: the points and the line on a floor; at each point a small bell stands up out of the plane centred on the line, its height at the data point = that point's likelihood; drag the line (w, b) — the product of heights (log-lik) and the SSE move in lockstep; toggle Gaussian/Laplace noise and add an outlier to compare the two fits. Checks. Drawer: −log of the Gaussian likelihood.

**Act IV — scores into probabilities, and measuring surprise**
- **s10 · Softmax: turning scores into probabilities.** exp makes everything positive and keeps the order; divide by the total. Scores (2, 1, 0) → 0.665, 0.245, 0.090. Shift-invariance (subtract the max — why code does it); temperature T: T = 0.5 → 0.867, 0.117, 0.016; T = 2 → 0.506, 0.307, 0.186; T → 0 argmax, T → ∞ uniform (this is the dial LLMs expose — Unit 19). Two classes: softmax = sigmoid of the difference (Unit 13's f(x) finally gets a probability). **w-softmax**: draggable score bars → probability bars, T slider (log scale), a "subtract the max" toggle that shows nothing changes. **w-simplex (3-D)**: the probability triangle (simplex) in 3-D; the current softmax output is a glowing point on it; sweeping T draws its path from the corner (T → 0) to the centre (T → ∞). Checks. Drawer: shift invariance; 2-class softmax = σ(z₁ − z₂).
- **s11 · Surprise and entropy.** Surprise of an outcome = −log p (rare → big surprise; certain → none). Entropy = average surprise. Fair coin 1 bit; 0.9/0.1 coin ≈ 0.469 bits; uniform over 4 ⇒ 2 bits; nats vs bits (ln vs log₂). A toss before a cricket match vs guessing tomorrow's sunrise. **w-entropy**: binary entropy curve with a draggable p; a 3-outcome version shown as an entropy heat map on the simplex with a draggable point. Checks.
- **s12 · Cross-entropy and KL: paying for the wrong belief.** If the truth is p but you plan for q, your average surprise is H(p, q) = H(p) + KL(p‖q). p = (0.5, 0.5), q = (0.9, 0.1): H(p,q) ≈ 1.737 bits, KL(p‖q) ≈ 0.737 bits, KL(q‖p) ≈ 0.531 bits — not symmetric, never negative, zero only when q = p. **w-kl**: two draggable bar distributions p and q (up to 5 outcomes); H(p), H(p,q), KL both directions as stacked bars; a second tab fits one Gaussian q to a two-humped p by minimising KL(p‖q) (spreads over both humps) vs KL(q‖p) (locks onto one hump) — the owner's "compare the variants". Checks. Drawer: H(p,q) = H(p) + KL(p‖q); why KL ≥ 0 (in outline — Jensen).
- **s13 · Cross-entropy is the classifier's likelihood.** With a one-hot label the cross-entropy is just −log q(correct class) — the negative log-likelihood of s7 again. Scores (2,1,0), true class 1st: loss = −ln 0.665 ≈ 0.408 nats; gradient w.r.t. the scores = q − y = (−0.335, 0.245, 0.090) — "prediction minus truth", the cleanest gradient in machine learning and the first step of every backward pass in Unit 15. **w-train (flagship)**: a live softmax (multinomial logistic) classifier on 2-D, 3-class data trained by gradient descent (Unit 9); decision regions shaded by probability (soft edges), a draggable probe point with its three probabilities; loss curve; step / run / reset; temperature at prediction time. Checks. Drawer: ∂(−log softmax)/∂z = q − y.
- **s14 · What to carry forward.** Summary card (distributions, Bayes, the Gaussian and its ellipse, likelihood/MLE, least squares = Gaussian MLE, softmax + T, entropy / cross-entropy / KL, q − y) + the thread forward: Unit 15 wires softmax + cross-entropy onto a whole network; Unit 16 predicts the next word with a softmax; Unit 19 samples with temperature; Unit 20 uses the Gaussian, the reparameterisation and KL.
